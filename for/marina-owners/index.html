Your day on the water

Start with what you need to do.

  1. 01

    Put your marina on its own map

    Claim your marina and, once approved, maintain the information your team and visitors use. Draw docks and berths over the available imagery. Record dimensions, supplied depth information, entry direction, tie side and facilities where known.

    The layout becomes a working reference for the people making assignments and the people meeting vessels on the dock.

  2. 02

    Connect a request to a stay

    Handle incoming requests and staff-entered stays. Review arrivals and departures, assign suitable berths and record when vessels come alongside or leave.

    Keep customer records available to the colleagues who need them. Length-based rate cards support marina stay quotes, while the dock team handles the physical berth assignment.

  3. 03

    Keep residents and visitors clear

    Record resident berths, absence dates and closures. An empty-looking berth may still belong to a resident or be reserved for courtesy use. Availability needs to reflect those arrangements.

    Where a resident's berth may be offered during an absence, record the permission separately from the dates they are away.

  4. 04

    Give the team the right tools

    Set access for dockmasters, front-desk colleagues and other staff. Check who is on duty, use the staff conversation and keep an incident record the team can refer to.

How access works

Owners and administrators manage the workspace. Dockmaster, front desk, accounts, auditor, staff and viewer roles give the team different responsibilities. Finance and several planning boards remain in development.

Available now and developing

Core berth, stay, resident and staff workflows are implemented. Advanced reservation boards, marina finance, payments and broader reporting are still being developed. A walkthrough can establish what fits your operation now.
